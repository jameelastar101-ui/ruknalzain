Stable flooring advice written for northern Europe assumes cold slabs, frost and
mud. The UAE gives you the opposite problem set: a concrete floor that bakes,
coastal humidity that keeps bedding and ammonia damp, fine dust that gets into
every joint, and wash-downs that have to drain fast in the heat. The flooring
choices that work here are shaped by those conditions.

## The base you build on matters more than the mat

Whatever surface goes on top, it is only as good as what is under it. Most UAE
stables are built on a concrete slab; some use a compacted sand or aggregate
base. Either way, before any matting goes down:

- **Level and smooth.** The surface under the mats must be flat. High spots
  create rocking points that wear the mat and bruise hooves; hollows collect
  liquid.
- **Fall to a drain.** Build in a slight, consistent slope — roughly 1:60 to
  1:80 — toward a channel or gully so urine and wash water leave quickly instead
  of sitting under the mat in the heat.
- **Free-draining blinding.** Over a sand or aggregate base, finish with a
  well-graded crushed stone blinded with stone dust and compacted hard, so the
  mat is fully supported.
- **Dry before you cover.** Trapped construction moisture plus a sealed rubber
  top is how you get persistent smell and a slippery underside.

Get this wrong and no mat will save you. Get it right and a mid-range mat will
outperform an expensive one laid on a poor base.

## Rubber stall mats: the default interior choice

For the stall itself, dense recycled-rubber matting is the standard, and for good
reason in this climate:

- **Thermal buffer.** Rubber breaks the contact between the horse and a slab that
  swings from hot in the afternoon to cool overnight. The horse lies down on a
  stable surface temperature.
- **Joint and tendon comfort.** A resilient floor reduces concussion for a horse
  standing on hard ground for much of the day.
- **Grip.** A textured rubber surface gives confident footing for getting up and
  down, wet or dry — unlike sealed concrete or tiles.
- **Less bedding.** A cushioned, sealed floor needs a fraction of the shavings a
  bare slab does, which in a region that imports bedding is a running cost that
  adds up.
- **Durability.** Recycled SBR rubber shrugs off hooves, barrows and daily
  mucking out for years.

**Choosing the mat for UAE conditions:**

| Requirement | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- |
| Dense, heavy grade | Stays put without curling; withstands high stall traffic |
| Interlocking or tight butt joints | Stops urine tracking under the mat in humid air |
| UV-stable compound | Essential for any mat near a stable door or outside |
| Textured, non-polished top | Grip stays reliable during frequent wash-downs |
| Wash-friendly surface | Lets you disinfect quickly in a climate that grows bacteria fast |

## Hygiene routine, not just product

Because heat and humidity accelerate bacterial and ammonia build-up, the floor
system has to be maintainable:

1. **Muck out fully** and let the surface air where possible.
2. **Lift and wash mats** on a schedule — monthly is a reasonable starting point,
   more often for problem stalls — and scrub and disinfect the sub-base beneath.
3. **Check seams and edges** for lifting or gaps and re-seat mats before liquid
   starts tracking under them.
4. **Keep air moving.** Cross-ventilation or fans clear the ammonia that
   otherwise sits at floor level in still, humid conditions.

## Outdoor: turnout, gateways and wash bays

The areas that fail first in a UAE yard are the high-traffic outdoor zones —
gateways, the strip in front of the barn, wash bays, walker approaches. Bare
ground turns to dust or mud; concrete and stone get hot and slick.

Perforated rubber pavers and interlocking outdoor mats solve this:

- they **drain** through the surface instead of pooling;
- they stay **cooler underfoot** than exposed concrete or pavers in direct sun;
- they give **grip when wet**;
- they **stabilise** the ground so it does not rut or erode.

## A simple specification

For a typical UAE competition or private yard:

- **Stalls:** dense interlocking rubber mats over a level, sealed, drained slab.
- **Aisles and wash bays:** heavy-duty hammer-top matting or ribbed rubber, laid
  to fall.
- **Gateways and turnout approaches:** perforated rubber pavers on a compacted
  free-draining base.
- **Everywhere:** ventilation, a drainage fall, and a lift-and-wash routine.
