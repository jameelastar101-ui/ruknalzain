## Introduction: Why Quality Horse Mats Matter in the UAE

A horse mat is a thick rubber floor that sits on top of concrete in a stall or stable. For most stalls in Dubai, a 17–20 mm mat is the best choice. For lighter areas like walkways, a 14 mm mat is enough.

Concrete is hard, hot and slippery when wet. Horses spend many hours on their feet, so the floor matters. A good rubber mat protects their hooves and joints. It also keeps the stable cleaner and easier to manage.

Here is what quality horse mats can do for your stable:

- **Comfort:** a soft surface that reduces tired legs
- **Hoof and joint support:** less shock on every step
- **Hygiene:** a surface that is easy to wash and disinfect
- **Safety:** better grip, even when the floor is wet
- **Less bedding:** you use less shavings or straw
- **Easier cleaning:** manure and urine are simple to remove

This guide is for horse owners, stable managers, equestrian centres, farms and trainers in Dubai, Sharjah and across the UAE. You will learn what horse mats are, how to pick the right one, how to install and clean them, and how to avoid costly mistakes.

## What Are Horse Mats?

Horse mats are heavy-duty rubber flooring for stalls, stables, wash bays and walkways. They sit on a hard base such as concrete. They give horses a softer, safer and cleaner place to stand.

Horse mats need higher density, better grip and more strength under hoof traffic. Gym mats are made for dropped weights, not hooves, moisture and urine. A thin gym mat can crack, compress or turn slippery in a stall.

## Benefits of Horse Mats

Horse mats make stables safer, cleaner and cheaper to run. Here are the main benefits:

1. **Non-slip footing.** A textured rubber surface helps horses keep their balance, even on wet days.
2. **Cushioning and less fatigue.** Rubber absorbs shock, so legs and hooves feel less strain.
3. **Thermal insulation.** Rubber keeps horses off hard, hot concrete, which helps them rest more comfortably.
4. **Easier manure and urine management.** Mats give you a flat, firm surface to clean quickly.
5. **Lower bedding use.** With a soft base, you need less bedding to keep the stall comfortable.
6. **Subfloor protection.** Mats shield the concrete from hoof damage and moisture.
7. **Better hygiene and odour control.** A smooth, sealed surface is easier to wash and disinfect.

### Is the Surface Non-Slip When Wet?

Good horse mats have a textured top that grips hooves. Smooth rubber can turn slippery when wet. For wash bays and aisles, always choose a textured surface.

### Will Horse Mats Hurt My Horse's Hooves or Joints?

No. A well-chosen mat does the opposite. Rubber cushions each step and reduces the shock that travels up the leg. The mat should be firm enough to support the horse, not so soft that the hooves sink. A layer of bedding on top adds comfort.

### Are Horse Mats Safe Around Horses?

Choose mats made from non-toxic rubber with no harmful chemicals. Rubber does not splinter like wood. Good mats also have smooth edges and do not crack into sharp pieces. Ask your supplier to confirm this.

## Types of Horse Mats

There are six common types of horse mats. Each one suits a different job.

- **Solid rubber stall mats:** flat, dense mats for everyday stall use
- **Interlocking rubber tiles:** smaller tiles that lock together and are easy to move
- **Perforated or drainage mats:** mats with holes that let liquid pass through
- **Grooved-bottom mats:** mats with channels underneath that help liquid flow away
- **Custom-cut stable flooring:** mats cut to fit odd-shaped areas
- **Portable trailer mats:** lighter mats made for trailers and travel

## How to Choose the Right Horse Mat

The right horse mat depends on thickness, size, weight, grip, drainage and climate. Check each point below before you buy.

- **Thickness:** Choose 14 mm for light use or walkways. Choose 17–20 mm for stalls and busy areas.
- **Size and coverage:** Zain horse mats measure 122 × 183 cm (about 6 ft × 4 ft). Cut them to fit if your stall has an odd shape.
- **Weight:** Heavier mats stay in place and are less likely to curl.
- **Surface texture:** A grippy surface is vital, especially when the floor is wet.
- **Density and durability:** A dense mat resists hoof traffic, cracking and compression.
- **Drainage:** Solid mats suit stalls with a sloped floor. Perforated mats suit wet areas.
- **Odour and safety:** Look for low-odour rubber, non-toxic materials and smooth edges.
- **Climate suitability:** Make sure the mat copes with Dubai heat, humidity and regular wash-downs.

**Tip:** If you are unsure, send your stall size and use to your supplier. A good supplier will help you pick the right thickness.

### What Is the Difference Between 14 mm, 17 mm and 20 mm Mats?

A 14 mm mat (29 kg) suits walkways and light-use areas. A 17 mm (41 kg) or 20 mm (56 kg) mat suits stalls where horses stand for long periods. Thicker mats give more cushioning and last longer, but they cost more.

### What Sizes Are Available?

Each Zain horse mat measures 122 × 183 cm (about 6 ft × 4 ft), which covers 2.23 m². Weight depends on thickness: 29 kg at 14 mm, 41 kg at 17 mm and 56 kg at 20 mm. Tolerances are ±3 mm on width and length and ±2 mm on thickness.

### Can the Mats Be Cut to Fit My Stable?

Yes. Rubber mats can be trimmed with a sharp utility knife and a straight edge. If you do not want to cut them yourself, ask whether the supplier offers custom cutting.

### How Many Mats Do I Need?

Divide the stall area by the area of one mat. Then add a few extra for cuts and for fitting around walls. The installation guide below has a worked example.

### Do Horse Mats Smell?

New rubber can have a rubbery smell at first. Good ventilation helps it fade. The time it takes depends on the product, so ask your supplier. Low-odour rubber is the best choice for closed stables.

### Are Horse Mats Suitable for Dubai's Heat?

Zain horse mats have a working temperature of up to 70 °C. In direct sun, any dark rubber can get hot to the touch. A shaded stable is the safest place for rubber mats.

### Will Horse Mats Fade, Crack or Curl?

Quality mats stand up to moisture, hoof traffic and regular cleaning. Strong UV light can age low-grade rubber, so ask about UV resistance. Heavier, denser mats are less likely to curl.

### How Long Do Horse Mats Last?

Life span depends on thickness, traffic and how well you maintain the mats. Be careful with "lifetime" claims. Ask for a realistic life span and the warranty terms in writing.

### Can Horse Mats Be Used Outdoors?

Only if the mat is made for outdoor use. Even then, pick a shaded spot with good drainage so water does not pool on the surface. ## Installation Guide

You can install horse mats on a clean, level, hard base in a few simple steps. Good prep makes the job faster and the floor last longer. For more on installing mats, read <a href="https://thehorse.com/135670/installing-rubber-stall-mats/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Installing Rubber Stall Mats</a> on The Horse.

**Pre-installation checklist**

- Measure the area
- Clear out bedding, manure and debris
- Check that the base is dry, flat and firm
- Plan where the drainage will flow
- Have a sharp utility knife and a straight edge ready

**Step by step**

1. **Prepare the base.** Concrete, compacted gravel or another level hard surface works well. Fix cracks and holes first. A soft or uneven base makes mats shift and wear out faster.
2. **Measure the stall.** Measure the length and width in feet or metres. Note any corners, doors or drains.
3. **Work out how many mats you need.** Divide the stall area by the area of one mat. A 6 ft × 4 ft mat covers 24 sq ft. A 12 ft × 12 ft stall (144 sq ft) needs 6 mats. Order a little extra for cuts.
4. **Cut the mats safely.** Mark the line with a straight edge. Use a sharp utility knife and make several light passes. Wear gloves and cut away from your body.
5. **Fit the mats tightly.** Push the edges together so there are no gaps. Gaps let urine seep underneath and let mats slide.
6. **Manage drainage and slope.** If the floor slopes to a drain, lay the mats so liquid can flow that way.
7. **Check if you need adhesive or fixing.** Heavy, tight-fitting mats often stay put on their own. In wet or busy areas, ask your supplier whether fixing is needed.

**DIY or professional installation?**

DIY works for small, simple stalls. A professional is a better choice for large areas, odd shapes or drainage work. An expert can also make sure the cuts are clean and the fit is tight.

### Can I Install Horse Mats Over Existing Concrete?

Yes, if the concrete is level, clean and dry. Repair cracks and holes first. Mats on uneven or soft ground shift and wear out early.

### Do the Mats Need to Be Glued or Fixed Down?

It depends on the product. Heavy mats often stay in place by weight. Interlocking tiles lock together. Some wet or busy areas may need fixing, so ask your supplier before you start.

### How Do I Prevent Gaps and Movement?

Fit the mats tightly, edge to edge. Prepare the base well. Place cut edges against the walls so the factory edges meet in the middle.

### Can I Install the Mats Myself?

Yes, for small and simple stalls. For bigger areas, ask the Rukn Al Zain team about professional installation in your Emirate.

## Cleaning and Maintenance

Rubber stable mats are easy to clean if you follow a simple routine. Regular care keeps the stall fresh and helps the mats last longer.

**Daily routine**

1. Remove manure and wet bedding.
2. Sweep the mat clean.
3. Hose it down.
4. Use a mild detergent or a stable-safe disinfectant when needed.
5. Let the mat dry before you add fresh bedding.

**Keep urine and odour under control**

Urine can seep under mats if there are gaps. This causes strong ammonia smells. Keep joints tight and lift the mats now and then to check underneath. Ammonia irritates a horse's eyes and airways, so good hygiene matters; <a href="https://www.yourhorse.co.uk/horse-care/vet-advice/horse-ammonia-health-risk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Your Horse explains the health risk</a>.

**Periodic deep cleaning**

Once in a while, lift the mats. Wash and dry the floor beneath them. Scrub the mat surface to remove built-up dirt.

**Inspect often**

Check for:

- Wear and thin spots
- Curling edges
- Gaps between mats
- Cracks or other damage

### Do Horse Mats Drain Urine and Water?

Solid mats do not drain through. Grooved undersides can help move moisture. Real drainage comes from the stall base and its slope.

### Will Urine Get Under the Mats?

It can, if there are gaps. To reduce this, fit the mats tightly, use absorbent bedding, clean often and make sure the floor slopes the right way.

### Are Horse Mats Waterproof?

Rubber does not soak up water, and the top surface sheds it. But water can still pool if drainage is poor, so check the slope of your floor.

### How Do I Control Ammonia and Odour?

Use absorbent bedding and clean every day. Keep the stable well ventilated: <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3117214" target="_blank" rel="noopener">a Swedish study of a riding-school stable</a> found that better air exchange lowered carbon dioxide, ammonia and horse allergen levels. Lift the mats now and then to wash and dry the floor underneath.

## Cost and Budgeting

The price of a horse mat depends mainly on its thickness, size, material and quantity. Delivery and installation add to the total.

**What changes the price**

- Thickness (thicker mats cost more)
- Mat size and weight
- Type of rubber
- Order quantity
- Delivery distance
- Installation

**Price per square metre vs. price per mat**

Always compare the cost per square metre, not just the cost per mat. Mats come in different sizes, so a lower price per mat may cover less floor. Divide the price by the area to compare fairly.

**Why cheap mats can cost more later**

Thin or low-quality mats can crack, curl or flatten. They may also smell strongly or get slippery. You then have to replace them sooner. A solid mat that lasts for years is often the better deal.

**Delivery and installation**

Delivery charges can differ between Dubai, Sharjah, Abu Dhabi and the other Emirates. Ask for the delivery cost and timeline in writing. For installation, ask what is included, such as cutting, fitting and clean-up.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most horse mat problems come from seven easy-to-avoid mistakes:

1. **Buying mats that are too thin.** Thin mats wear out fast and give little cushioning in stalls.
2. **Installing on an uneven or soft base.** The mats will shift, dip and crack.
3. **Leaving gaps between mats.** Urine and dirt collect underneath.
4. **Ignoring drainage and floor slope.** Liquid pools and causes odour.
5. **Choosing smooth mats for wet areas.** Horses can slip.
6. **Not cleaning under the mats.** Ammonia builds up and harms hygiene.
7. **Ordering without checking delivery and installation terms.** Hidden costs and delays can follow.
